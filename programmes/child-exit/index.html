CHILD-EXIT

From immediate protection to sustainable exit.

CHILD-EXIT is a child-focused, longitudinal research framework developed by CIRITS under the project direction of Cyril Malglaive. It examines how separation from child criminal exploitation and forced criminality may — or may not — become a sustainable exit over time.

“Removal is an event. Sustainable exit is a process.”

The research gap

Removal does not necessarily mean exit.

Children exploited through criminal activities may be identified, removed from immediate danger and recognised as victims while remaining exposed to the functions and conditions that can make re-recruitment, re-exploitation or renewed dependence possible.

Under what conditions does separation from forced criminality become a sustainable exit for children and adolescents, and through which mechanisms is that durability produced, weakened or reversed?

CHILD-EXIT does not claim that the general idea of sustainable exit is new. Its specific contribution is to formalise a child-focused, longitudinal and falsifiable research architecture linking protective separation, functional replacement, identity transition, social recognition and reincorporation, ecological and institutional continuity, and sustainability over time.

The CHILD-EXIT architecture

Seven mechanisms to understand how exit becomes sustainable

The architecture is dynamic rather than linear. Oscillation, re-contact, setbacks and renewed protection are treated as data about the trajectory, not automatically coded as failure.

01 · Protective separation Reduced coercive control and immediate safety.
02 · Liminal transition A fragile in-between phase marked by instability and uncertainty.
03 · Functional replacement Replacing the functions previously supplied by the criminal environment.
04 · Identity transition Making a different trajectory imaginable and enactable.
05 · Recognition & reincorporation Social recognition of change and re-entry into ordinary life.
06 · Ecological & institutional continuity Stable support across environments, relationships and systems.
07 · Longitudinal sustainability Whether the exit remains viable over time.

Longitudinal research programme

What must now be tested over time?

01

At 12 months: has the child remained outside the exploitative environment, and what supports have replaced the functions previously supplied by the network?

02

At 24 months: has social recognition of change taken hold, and are education, care, work or daily routines becoming credible alternatives?

03

At 36 months: does the exit remain viable, which mechanisms appear strongest in sustaining change, and what weakens or reverses durability?

Research foundations

Connected to the broader CIRITS research programme

CHILD-EXIT is developed in relation to CIRITS work on sustainable exit and on the longitudinal trajectories of documented change after intensive protection or intervention.

A complementary strategic study, NO EXIT — Evidence for Action, examines the cumulative human, institutional and economic costs that can arise when protection does not become a sustainable exit.

Scientific position

A developing research framework, not an established standard

CHILD-EXIT Version 1.0 is now a public, attributable and citable research framework. It is not presented as a validated intervention, a standardised protocol or a model whose effectiveness has already been demonstrated.

CIRITS develops the framework as a research proposition to be confronted with international scientific literature, professional practice, public policy and empirical evaluation.

Its development relies on dialogue with researchers, child-protection professionals, anti-trafficking actors, justice institutions and international organisations.
